# 03 — Restriction map: prevalence, severity, and how well eating out is served

*Source: research part 1.2 and 1.3.*

## The map

| Category | Prevalence (best source found) | Severity | Existing service when eating out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Food allergy (IgE)** | ~4.3% worldwide, 7.6% in the US (Forero Molina et al., *Immunol Allergy Clin N Am*, 2025); about 1 in 10 adults and 1 in 12 children; ~2.4 million in the UK (FSA) | **Life-threatening** (anaphylaxis); a trace amount is enough | Medium. Spokin and EveryBite, mostly US |
| **Celiac disease** | 1.4% by serology, 0.7% by biopsy (Singh et al. meta-analysis, 2018); most patients undiagnosed | Autoimmune damage, also from trace amounts; cross-contact matters | The best understood (Find Me Gluten Free), **but at restaurant level, not dish level** |
| **Non-celiac gluten sensitivity (NCGS)** | Not known; disputed | Discomfort | Treated as part of the "gluten-free" category |
| **Lactose intolerance** | ~68% of adults worldwide with lactose malabsorption; ~36% in the US (NIDDK; Storhaug et al., *Lancet Gastro* 2017 — **note: that meta-analysis was later retracted, so treat the figure as order-of-magnitude only**) | Discomfort | Weak. Treated as "dairy-free", confused with milk allergy |
| **FODMAP/IBS, histamine, fructose, salicylates, sulphites, MSG, nightshades, caffeine** | IBS is very common; data scattered | Discomfort (sulphites can trigger asthma) | **Almost unhandled in restaurants.** The Monash app and Fig focus on packaged products |
| **GLP-1 users (small portions, protein)** | 11% of US adults in 2026 (Gallup, June 2026, n=5,065), up from 3% in 2024 | A preference / medical state, no danger | **Not handled at all.** A Toast consumer survey (August 2026, n=850 GLP-1 users): 87% order from a restaurant at least weekly, 72% have chosen a restaurant for its small-appetite options, 47% are "strongly" influenced by protein grams being shown |
| **Diabetes, low-carb and keto** | Diabetes prevalence very high worldwide | Medical / discomfort | Weak. **This is KetoClub's existing asset** |
| **Kidney (potassium, phosphorus, sodium), gout, GERD, IBD, EoE, PKU, pregnancy** | Varies | Medical; in PKU and advanced kidney disease there is real danger | Not handled. Needs nutritional data that almost never exists for restaurant food |
| **Vegan / vegetarian / pescatarian** | Large (HappyCow reports millions of users) | Choice / identity | Served well at restaurant level (HappyCow: 240,000+ listings in 185 countries) |
| **Kosher, halal, Jain** | Large sectors worldwide | Religious | Partial. Kosher and halal are essentially a question of **certification**, which an LLM cannot verify — it can only flag components (pork, meat with dairy) |
| **Paleo, Whole30, AIP, Mediterranean** | Niche | Lifestyle | Weak, but easy to serve with rules |

## Market size — handle with care

Estimates of the 2025 "free-from" market range from about **$35 billion
(Dataintelo) to $157 billion (MRFR)**, with IMARC near $95 billion and
Mordor near $114 billion. A spread like that means different definitions,
so these numbers are an indicator of growth, not a basis for a business
plan. A more reliable sizing would be **bottom-up** — from user counts, e.g.
the competitors' download numbers (file 04).

## Priorities the research derives (part 1.3)

- **Largest segment:** lactose, then gluten/allergies, then vegan.
- **Fastest growing:** GLP-1 (×3.7 in two years in the US), alpha-gal, and
  adult diagnoses of celiac and allergies.
- **Least served when eating out:** everything outside the big nine
  (mustard, legumes), FODMAP, histamine, GLP-1, alpha-gal — and **any
  combination of several restrictions**.
- **Recommended order:**
  1. The EU 14 and the US 9, celiac, lactose, vegan/vegetarian, keto/low-carb.
  2. FODMAP, histamine, GLP-1, alpha-gal, legumes.
  3. Medical diets only with a clinical partner (kidney, PKU, diabetes);
     kosher/halal **components** (never certification).
