# 02 — Regulated allergens by jurisdiction

*Source: research part 1.1.*

Every jurisdiction shares the Codex base of **eight** allergens: peanuts,
tree nuts, milk, eggs, soy, wheat, fish, crustaceans. The differences are in
the additions, and they decide what the product's taxonomy has to look like.

## The lists

| Jurisdiction (law) | Count | Added beyond the Codex eight | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **United States** (FALCPA + FASTER Act) | 9 | Sesame (since January 2023) | No duty to label molluscs, mustard, celery or lupin |
| **European Union** (FIC, Regulation 1169/2011) | 14 | Gluten-containing cereals, celery, mustard, sesame, lupin, molluscs, sulphites above 10 mg/kg | Applies to non-prepacked food too — i.e. **restaurants** |
| **United Kingdom** | 14 | Same as the EU | Natasha's Law covers food prepacked for direct sale |
| **Canada** | 10 + gluten + sulphites | Mustard, molluscs, sesame | Bilingual labelling (English/French) |
| **Australia / New Zealand** (PEAL) | ~12–13 | Sesame, lupin, molluscs, sulphites; tree nuts named by type | A "Contains" statement is mandatory |
| **Japan** | 8 mandatory + ~20 recommended | Buckwheat, crab, shrimp and walnut mandatory; cashew in transition; soy and sesame only recommended | Buckwheat, critical in Japan, is on no Western list. Recommended list includes kiwi, banana, apple, peach, beef, chicken, pork, salmon, salmon roe, mackerel, gelatin and more |
| **Israel** | aligned to the EU list (the same 14) | Allergens emphasised inside the ingredient list; a "fava beans — not suitable for G6PD-deficient" marking | Voluntary from 1 Jan 2025, **mandatory from 1 Jan 2028 — for pre-packaged food only.** The 2022 draft defers non-pre-packaged (restaurant) food to "a later stage"; no restaurant duty was found (file 12 §4) |

## What it means for the product

- The taxonomy must hold the **union of all lists — about 30 items** — each
  tagged with the jurisdictions in which it is regulated. A "trip mode" then
  knows what the local restaurant is *obliged* to disclose and what the user
  must *ask* about instead. Examples: mustard allergy in the US (not
  regulated there), buckwheat in Japan (regulated only there), lupin in
  France (regulated in the EU, used in flour).
- Israel's own list is EU-shaped, which makes the EU 14 the natural home
  list for the pilot market. **Correction after file 12:** the 2028 rule
  reaches packaged food only; Israeli restaurant allergen information is
  voluntary today and no date for a restaurant duty was found. The
  tailwind is in what chains *could* publish from their packaged-ingredient
  labels, not in what they must.

## Less common allergens worth supporting

- **Alpha-gal syndrome** (mammalian meat allergy after a tick bite). Two
  CDC MMWR reports (July 2023): over 110,000 suspected cases identified
  2010–2022, up to 450,000 people in the US may be affected, and 42% of
  1,500 surveyed physicians had never heard of it. Spreading geographically.
- **Oral allergy syndrome (OAS)** — raw fruits and vegetables cross-reacting
  with pollens.
- **Corn.**
- **Legumes** — lentils, peas, chickpeas. Critical in the Mediterranean and
  in Israel (hummus, falafel, every salad bar).
- **Seeds** — sunflower, flax.
- **Kiwi.**
- **Meats.**
