# 01 — Executive summary

*Source: the uploaded research, title page and TL;DR.*

## The bottom line

**Expand — but not as "another allergy app".** The niche of analysing a
*specific dish* on a *specific menu*, for a *profile that combines several
restrictions*, in *any language*, is still untaken. The veteran players each
cover one restriction and lean on crowd reviews; the new AI apps are small,
undifferentiated and give no reliable explanation for their answers.
KetoClub's advantage would be the **reliability and transparency of the
engine**, not the breadth of its restriction list.

## TL;DR

1. **The audience is large and fragmented.** Food allergy affects about
   4.3% of the world population (Forero Molina et al., 2025), about 8% of
   children and 10% of adults in developed countries (JACI reviews
   2021–2023). Celiac disease reaches 1.4% by serology. Add vegans, GLP-1
   patients (11% of US adults in 2026 per Gallup), medical diets and
   religious diets. **No single app serves a combination of restrictions
   at dish level, outside the US, in many languages.**
2. **The competitors' weakness is structural.** Find Me Gluten Free and
   HappyCow cover one restriction each and are review-based. Fig and Spokin
   are strong mainly in the supermarket and in the US. AllergyEats is nearly
   dead. On the delivery platforms, about 80% of the restaurants checked
   offer only a free-text notes box. The new AI apps (MenuSafe, Forkiee,
   SnackPro, Allergy Lens and others) promise "scan any menu" but with no
   validation, no confidence score and no clinical data model.
3. **The recommendation:** a combined profile with severity levels; a
   hybrid engine (LLM ingredient extraction, a deterministic rules layer on
   top, and official chain allergen data where it exists); "questions for
   the waiter" and a translated card in every language; an explained
   confidence score per dish. **Phase 1 scope:** the EU's 14 allergens and
   the US's 9, celiac, lactose, vegan/vegetarian, and keto/low-carb (what
   exists today). **Revenue:** premium at roughly $4–8 a month, and a B2B
   channel to restaurants later.

## What this means in one sentence

KetoClub already has the hard part — a per-dish verdict with a modification
to read to the waiter, in the menu's own language, from an LLM with a
deterministic fallback — and the research says that exact shape is what the
allergy, intolerance and diet market lacks.
