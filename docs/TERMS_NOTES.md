# Terms and listing notes (Banto)

Working notes for whoever writes the terms of use and the App Store / Play
listings. This is the product stance and the clauses to carry, not legal
drafting.

## Product stance

- Banto is a **helper tool**. It reads menus to help a person choose.
- It **cannot see the kitchen**, verify a recipe, or know about cross-contact,
  substitutions or last-minute changes. It **promises no accuracy**.
- The user must **always ask the restaurant** about anything that matters to
  them. The app says so wherever a result appears (`helperToolSentence`,
  `helperToolTitle` in the ARB files).
- **No severity levels.** Every restriction is treated the same; the app never
  ranks one as "mild" or "serious".
- **No medical claim.** The app does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent
  anything and is not a medical device. `test/l10n/banned_words_test.dart`
  keeps that vocabulary out of the in-app copy, in both languages.
- **The restriction profile never leaves the device**, not even as ids
  (architecture.md §11, "Banto (C1)"). Only menu text and the keto settings are
  sent when AI analysis is on (`whatLeavesDeviceBanto`).

## Clauses the terms of use should carry

- The service is an informational aid and is provided as is, without warranty
  of accuracy, completeness or fitness for a purpose.
- Results are generated by software from the menu text a platform or the user
  supplies; they can be wrong or out of date.
- The user is responsible for confirming ingredients and preparation with the
  restaurant before ordering. Nothing in the app replaces asking.
- The app gives no medical, dietary or health advice and no diagnosis.
- No liability for a choice made on the basis of a result, to the extent the
  law allows.
- What data is sent, to whom and when (menu text to the backend and the AI
  provider; the profile stays on the device); how to turn AI analysis off.

## Clauses the store listings should carry

- Describe it as a tool that "helps you read menus". Do not describe it as
  safe, certified, verified or guaranteed.
- Put "Always ask the restaurant" in the description, not only in the app.
- Answer the store health and privacy questionnaires consistently with the
  above: no health data collected, the profile stays on the device.

## Store-policy risk

- Both stores restrict apps that appear to diagnose or treat a condition, or
  that present health claims they cannot substantiate. Wording such as
  "diagnose", "treat", "cure", "medically safe", "100% safe" or "guaranteed" in
  the listing, screenshots, review notes or the app itself is the risk.
- An app that reads as medical advice about allergies may also be reviewed as a
  medical or health-data app, which brings extra disclosure requirements.
  Keeping to the helper-tool stance avoids that category.

## Review

Counsel review of the terms and listings is recommended, not a release gate
(owner's decision, 2026-10-11).
